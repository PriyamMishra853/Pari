PARIKSHAK
SMART INDIA
HACKATHON

Problem Addressed During lunar and future BAS missions, communication delays and blackout periods prevent continuous ground supervision of scientific experiments.
Existing 2D posture models fail in microgravity because astronauts may work upright, sideways, or inverted, making experiment validation unreliable. Mission requirements demand a completely offline system that recognizes experiment steps, detects skipped or incorrect actions, guides the astronaut by voice, generates lightweight timestamped logs, and streams/stores video locally.
Proposed Solution PARIKSHAK is a standalone, air-gapped edge AI system installed inside payload racks. It continuously analyzes local video, recognizes experiment steps, guides astronauts by voice, detects skipped or incorrect actions, and records verified telemetry.
▤ Core Features & Implementation
📷 Autonomous On-board Edge: Processes fixed-payload camera feeds locally with zero internet, cloud, or ground control dependency.
👁 Multi-Modal Perception: YOLOv8n object detection + YOLOv8n-Pose (17 keypoints) + ContactMLP at ≥35 FPS.
⏱ Physical Evidence Gates: HSMM advances steps only after measurable proof: sustained grasp ≥1.4s, lift ≥30px, lid rotation.
🔊 Real-Time Voice Guidance: Offline TTS announces next authorized step & speaks immediate warnings for skipped/incorrect actions.
📐 Orientation-Agnostic 3D HMR: AprilTag 36h11 anchors rack-relative (+X, +Y, +Z) frame, tracking inverted or floating postures.
🔐 Cryptographic Flight Ledger: Automatically generates timestamped SHA-256 hash-chained JSONL logs, stores MP4 locally.

Risk and Strategy
Column 2: Potential Challenges & Risks (Space Environment)
Column 3: Strategies for Overcoming Challenges (Engineered Defenses)
1. Challenge 1: Zero-G Inverted Posture:
Astronauts float at arbitrary roll/pitch angles without an Earth-like floor reference.
Defense 1 (Rack-Relative 3D Normalization):
Normalizes all keypoints against static payload rack markers using `solvePnP`, ensuring invariant tracking whether the astronaut is floating upright or upside-down.
2. Challenge 2: Severe Tool/Hand Occlusion:
Glovebox frames, crew hands, and reagent containers frequently occlude facial keypoints and specimen vials.
Defense 2 (Geometric Fallback Cascade):
If mouth/eyes are occluded by bottles or pipettes, the engine estimates mouth position via an inter-pupillary midpoint and shoulder-vector offset, holding coordinates with a 10s decaying temporal cache.
3. Challenge 3: Specular Glare & Lighting Shifts:
Metallic rack walls and polycarbonate visor shields create harsh reflection hotspots.
Defense 3 (Adaptive CLAHE Pre-Processing):
Dynamic Contrast-Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization suppresses specular glare gradients and balances microgravity shadows.
4. Challenge 4: False Sequence Advancements:
Sensor noise or fleeting hand gestures could falsely advance steps and compromise scientific integrity.
Defense 4 (Strict Multi-Frame Accumulators):
Mandates sustained physical evidence (≥1.4s grasp holds, ≥1.0s release confirmation) and persistent deviation gating (1.4s, p ≥ 0.85), reducing false alarms to ≤0.85 per 45-minute procedure.
